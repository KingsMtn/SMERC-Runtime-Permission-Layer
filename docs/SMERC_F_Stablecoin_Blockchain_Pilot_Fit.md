# SMERC-F Stablecoin And Blockchain Pilot Fit

## Purpose

This document defines when stablecoin, tokenized-finance, blockchain, payment, or treasury organizations are a strong fit for SMERC-F.

SMERC-F should remain a financial-action governance spur, not a replacement for the core GitHub Actions pilot path. It is a second wedge for the right financial prospect, not a reason to abandon the first technical pilot wedge.

The core product path remains:

```text
AI-assisted engineering workflow -> GitHub Actions observe mode -> reviewer labels -> pilot evidence
```

The SMERC-F expansion path is:

```text
automated financial action -> recoverability scoring -> review/constraint/freeze decision -> evidence record
```

## Short Answer

Yes, companies planning stablecoin or blockchain financial workflows may be highly interested because those workflows often involve:

- irreversible or hard-to-reverse settlement
- fast autonomous execution
- treasury or liquidity movement
- wallet, custody, or counterparty concentration
- regulatory and audit sensitivity
- model- or agent-assisted operating decisions

However, they are not automatically the best first buyers. They become strong prospects only when they have automated financial actions with real side effects and a team willing to test SMERC-F in shadow mode.

## Strong-Fit Companies

Strong-fit SMERC-F prospects include:

- stablecoin issuers or infrastructure providers
- payment processors adding stablecoin settlement
- fintechs automating refunds, withdrawals, payouts, or treasury workflows
- crypto exchanges or custodians with automated withdrawal and treasury controls
- tokenized asset platforms
- banking innovation teams testing tokenized deposits or real-time settlement
- treasury operations teams using automation for liquidity movement
- risk teams overseeing autonomous financial workflows

## Weak-Fit Companies

Weak-fit prospects include:

- companies using blockchain only for marketing
- trading firms looking for price prediction
- crypto projects without enterprise controls
- teams that only need sanctions screening or KYC
- organizations unwilling to provide reviewer labels
- teams that expect SMERC-F to provide regulatory AML compliance
- teams without recoverability or rollback questions

## Why They May Care

Stablecoin and blockchain financial workflows make SMERC-F more intuitive because a bad action may be hard to reverse after execution.

Examples:

- A withdrawal is authorized but cannot be recalled.
- A treasury reallocation is internal but occurs during liquidity stress.
- A redemption passes identity checks but increases concentration risk.
- A refund batch is legitimate but too large to release automatically.
- A frozen payment has new evidence but still lacks enough confidence for release.

These are not only suspiciousness questions. They are recoverability and execution-governance questions.

## What To Test First

Start with metadata-only shadow-mode scoring. Do not connect SMERC-F to live funds, wallets, custody systems, settlement rails, or production financial execution during the first test.

Good first test actions:

- release payment
- hold payment
- approve refund batch
- release frozen payment
- approve stablecoin redemption
- approve digital-asset withdrawal
- change transaction limit
- rebalance treasury funds
- change counterparty exposure limit

## What The Prospect Provides

The prospect should provide metadata-only examples:

- action description
- actor or automation type
- action category
- current policy outcome where available
- expected review path
- reversibility estimate
- evidence-validity estimate
- authorization-support estimate
- settlement anomaly estimate
- liquidity or counterparty concentration estimate
- model disagreement or uncertainty estimate

The prospect should not provide production secrets, wallet keys, customer records, raw transaction payloads, private prompts, regulated records, or confidential suspicious-activity files without separate legal and security approval.

## What SMERC-F Returns

SMERC-F returns:

- `ALLOW`
- `THROTTLE`
- `FREEZE`
- `DENY`
- `ESCALATE`
- confidence
- signal risk
- irreversible exposure
- reversible capacity
- driver codes
- recommended controls
- replay identifier
- decision hash

## Buying Hypothesis

The highest-interest financial prospects are likely teams that already feel the pain of automated action but do not yet have a clean pre-execution governance layer.

The strongest buyer titles may be:

- CISO
- Head of Risk
- Head of Financial Crime Technology
- Head of Payments Risk
- Head of Treasury Operations
- Head of Blockchain/Stablecoin Infrastructure
- Chief Compliance Officer, if paired with a technical owner

The strongest first conversation is usually not:

> Do you need AI governance?

It is:

> Do you have automated financial actions where the action may be authorized but still too hard to reverse without added controls?

## Pilot Gate

Proceed to a SMERC-F pilot only if:

- financial actions are automated or becoming automated
- at least one action has meaningful irreversible exposure
- a reviewer group can label decisions
- the first test can run in shadow mode
- the metadata boundary is safe
- SMERC-F is treated as governance evidence, not financial-compliance certification

Do not proceed if the prospect wants regulatory AML replacement, trading signals, custody execution, settlement execution, or production blocking without a controlled pilot.

## Relationship To AML

AML systems ask whether activity is suspicious or reportable.

SMERC-F asks whether the proposed financial action is recoverable and defensible enough to execute automatically.

SMERC-F does not provide regulatory AML compliance, sanctions screening, suspicious-activity reporting, KYC, custody, settlement, or payment execution.

The strongest SMERC-F delta is:

```text
AML_CLEAR_SMERC_RESTRAINT
```

This means the action may not look suspicious under an AML-style baseline, but SMERC-F still recommends restraint because execution may be hard to reverse or operationally fragile.

## Recommended Sequence

1. Keep the GitHub Actions pilot as the primary product wedge.
2. Use the AML-inspired SMERC-F benchmark as a financial-domain proof.
3. Talk to financial/stablecoin prospects only with a narrow shadow-mode offer.
4. Ask for metadata-only action examples.
5. Compare SMERC-F posture against reviewer labels.
6. Build a financial pilot package only after one real financial reviewer confirms the use case is worth testing.

## Bottom Line

Stablecoin and blockchain financial companies may be among the most interested SMERC-F prospects, but only if they are already moving toward automated financial operations.

The fit is strongest where money-like actions are authorized by policy but still need recoverability-aware restraint before execution.
